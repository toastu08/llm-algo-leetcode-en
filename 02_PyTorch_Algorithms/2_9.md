# Group 2.9: Communication and Parallelism

This group organizes parallel mechanisms along two dimensions: “what to split” and “how to communicate after splitting”. Training state, layers, tensors, sequences, experts, and Serving requests are six parallel objects of division of labor; collective, payload, synchronization frequency, overlap, load imbalance, and scaling efficiency form a general communication evidence chain. The related mechanisms serve both distributed training and distributed inference.

## Group Overview

These mechanisms do not have a single linear order: 27 explains training-state sharding, 28 explains layers and stages, 29 explains matrix and Attention tensors, PAR-CONTEXT explains long-sequence splitting, 46 explains the dynamic dispatch of MoE tokens to experts, and PAR-REPLICA explains routing from Serving requests to model replicas. After choosing any division of labor, always return to `47 → 48 → 49` to establish the communication model, locate exposed hotspots, and compare combined strategies.

```text
Parallel splitting dimensions                   Communication analysis and decisions
├─ 27 Training-state sharding: ZeRO            47 Communication Profiling
├─ 28 Layer splitting: Pipeline Parallel       ↓
├─ 29 Tensor splitting: Tensor Parallel        48 Hotspot attribution and mitigation
├─ PAR-CONTEXT Sequence splitting: CP          ↓
├─ 46 Expert splitting: Expert Parallel        ↓
└─ PAR-REPLICA Request division: replica routing  49 Parallel composition and strategy selection
```

## Learning Path

### Recommended Order

- First choose a division dimension according to the problem: for training state see [27](./27_ZeRO_Optimizer_Sim.ipynb), for layer splitting see [28](./28_Pipeline_Parallelism_MicroBatch.ipynb), for tensor splitting see [29](./29_Tensor_Parallelism_Sim.ipynb), for long-sequence splitting see [PAR-CONTEXT](./PAR-CONTEXT_Context_and_Sequence_Parallelism.ipynb), for MoE expert splitting see [46](./46_MoE_Expert_Parallel.ipynb), and for Serving request distribution see [PAR-REPLICA](./PAR-REPLICA_Serving_Replica_Routing.ipynb). These mechanisms are parallel entries routed by problem.
- Then follow [47](./47_Communication_Profiling_with_NCCL.ipynb) → [48](./48_Communication_Hotspots_and_Mitigation.ipynb) → [49](./49_Parallelism_Strategy_Selection.ipynb) to establish the communication model, locate hotspots, and form a combined strategy. 46 enters only under MoE workload and is not a required page between 47 and 48.
- Project validation is routed by goal: [79](./79_Distributed_Parallel_Benchmark.ipynb) compares DDP / FSDP / ZeRO, [87 `PAR-MODEL-INTERNAL`](./87_Model_Internal_Parallel_Benchmark.ipynb) compares PP / TP / CP, [80](./80_MoE_Expert_Parallel_Benchmark.ipynb) validates EP, and [81](./81_Distributed_Inference_Project.ipynb) validates distributed inference and Serving replica routing. The four projects do not have to be completed in sequence.

### Next Steps

- After finishing this group, enter [2.10](./2_10.md) and choose a project according to the training-parallelism, MoE, or distributed-inference goal; completing 79–81 in sequence is not required.
- When the problem turns to the training memory ledger, review [43](./43_Unified_Training_Memory_Ledger.ipynb) and [75](./75_Memory_Budget_Compression_Project.ipynb); when it turns to kernel cost and autotuning, enter [Operator Optimization Task5](../topic_discussion/operator_optimization/05_cost_model_and_profiling.md).

## Environment Notes

- CPU mechanism experiments are used to understand splitting, state, and communication volume, and do not represent real scaling efficiency.
- collective, topology, overlap, and multi-GPU throughput require NCCL and at least two available GPUs for validation; the specific conditions are governed by the single-page notes.
